# Reply Pack

Short reply templates for Kestrel outreach.

Use these as starting points, not rigid scripts.
Keep them human, specific, and low-pressure.

---

## 1. If they say: "Sure, send it over"

Absolutely — here’s the short version of what stood out:

- <specific observation 1>
- <specific observation 2>
- <specific observation 3>

My general impression is that the business itself likely comes across stronger than the website does right now, and the opportunity is mostly in making trust, clarity, and next steps easier to absorb.

If useful, I can also do a fixed-scope **Credibility + Intake Review**. It’s a 2-business-day review of the key pages and booking / contact flow, with screenshots, prioritized fixes, and a short path-forward recommendation. Fixed price is **$1,500**.

— Daymian

---

## 2. If they ask: "What does that cost?"

For the review itself, it’s a fixed **$1,500**.

That includes:
- review of the key public-facing pages
- mobile + desktop inspection
- screenshots with callouts
- top issues ranked by practical impact
- a short 2-week / 4-week path forward

If the next step is implementation, I usually scope that after the review rather than pretending every project is identical.

---

## 3. If they ask: "What exactly would you look at?"

Usually:
- homepage first impression
- service / specialty clarity
- provider / team trust presentation
- new patient / intake clarity
- booking / contact flow
- mobile friction in the first key screens

The goal is not to produce a giant audit. It’s to identify the handful of changes most likely to make the site feel clearer, stronger, and easier to act on.

---

## 4. If they ask: "Can you just fix it?"

Possibly, yes.

The reason I like starting with the review is that it usually makes the implementation smaller, clearer, and easier to price honestly. Sometimes the right move is a credibility refresh. Sometimes it’s more about booking / intake cleanup. Sometimes there’s a small internal workflow issue underneath the website friction.

The review helps separate those.

---

## 5. If they ask for a call immediately

Happy to do that.

Before we talk, it may still help if I send over the short written observations first so the conversation has something concrete to react to. That usually makes the call more useful.

If you’d prefer, I can send that first and we can talk after.

---

## 6. If they ask whether this is a rebuild

Not necessarily.

Sometimes the right answer is a rebuild, but often the first useful move is narrower than that: tighten the trust story, simplify the booking path, improve mobile behavior, and clean up the pages that matter most.

I’d rather diagnose that honestly than jump straight to a rebuild pitch.

---

## 7. If they ask about timing

For the review:
- **2 business days** from kickoff and payment

For implementation, it depends on the shape of the work, but I usually try to keep first projects tight and legible rather than letting them sprawl.

---

## 8. If they ask about HIPAA / patient data

If anything touches patient data, forms, or intake records, I scope that around HIPAA-appropriate tooling and vendors.

The review itself stays on the public-facing / operational side and should not require access to protected health information.

---

## 9. If they go quiet after you send notes

Just following up in case the earlier note got buried.

My impression was still that there’s a practical opportunity to make the site feel stronger and easier to act on without turning it into a giant project.

If useful, I’m still happy to do the fixed-scope review and map out the order I’d tackle things.

---

## 10. If they say "not now"

Understood.

If it’s helpful later, I’m happy to revisit it with a fresh look. In the meantime, I appreciate the reply.

— Daymian

---

## 11. If they ask what happens after the review

Usually one of three things:
- a focused credibility / intake refresh
- a smaller booking / scheduling cleanup
- a process / tooling fix behind the scenes

And sometimes the answer is simply that no large project is necessary right now.

That’s part of why I prefer the review first — it keeps the next step honest.

---

## 12. Internal note on tone

The goal of these replies is not to "close hard." It is to:
- keep the conversation calm
- stay specific
- make the paid next step feel normal
- avoid turning Kestrel into a solicitor script
