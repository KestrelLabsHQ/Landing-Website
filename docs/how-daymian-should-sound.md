# How Daymian Should Sound

This document translates Daymian’s communication profile into practical guidance for future Kestrel writing.

Use it when drafting:
- outreach emails
- pitch language
- decks
- website copy
- brand positioning
- follow-ups
- short proposals

It is intended to answer:

> If Kestrel writing is meant to feel aligned with Daymian, what should it sound like?

---

## 1. Core tonal target

Daymian should sound like:
- calm
- real
- observant
- commercially aware
- understated
- specific
- not desperate
- not trying too hard

A good shorthand:

> calm competence with a pulse

Another good shorthand:

> sharp, real, and commercially useful without sounding sales-trained

---

## 2. Desired emotional impression

When someone reads Daymian/Kestrel writing, the ideal reaction is:
- this feels like a real person
- this person actually looked
- this person has taste
- this is thoughtful, not mass-produced
- this person understands business without sounding like a marketer cliché

Not:
- this is outreach automation
- this is an agency script
- this is overly polished
- this is trying too hard to be persuasive

---

## 3. Tone rules

### 3.1 Be specific, not broad
Prefer:
- one real observation
- one practical implication
- one believable upside

Avoid:
- generic praise
- generic diagnosis
- generic marketing promises

### 3.2 Be restrained, not timid
The writing should not sound weak or apologetic.
It should sound confident without pushing.

Good posture:
- “I noticed this.”
- “There may be an opportunity here.”
- “A few focused improvements could likely help.”

Avoid:
- “I’d love to help you scale…”
- “We can dramatically improve…”
- “Let’s jump on a quick call…”

### 3.3 Use opportunity framing more than criticism framing
Preferred framing:
- make the website work harder for the business
- make the site carry trust more clearly
- get more out of what already exists
- make next steps easier to act on

Less preferred framing:
- your site is old
- your site is weak
- your site has problems
- your site is hurting your business

### 3.4 Sound like a person, not a campaign
Writing should feel authored.
That means:
- fewer polished transitions
- fewer generic “business email” phrases
- less optimization-sounding language
- more natural rhythm

### 3.5 Keep local/founder signals subtle
Do not force “I’m local” into the body unless it feels truly natural.
Usually the better move is to let the signature imply groundedness.

Recommended signature structure:

<name>  
Kestrel Labs  
Atlanta, Georgia

## 4. Vocabulary preferences

### Preferred language
- stronger
- clearer
- sharper
- more effective
- more confidence-building
- more useful
- more aligned
- more representative of the business
- work harder for the business
- practical opportunity
- a few focused improvements
- if useful
- a few concise ideas

### Avoid language like
- online presence
- optimize conversions
- unlock growth
- elevate your brand
- scale your business
- cutting-edge
- transform your digital ecosystem
- growth strategy
- digital solutioning
- let’s book 15 minutes
- I’d love to help

---

## 5. Sentence style

### Good sentence qualities
- slightly understated
- natural rhythm
- compact
- believable
- precise without being stiff

### Bad sentence qualities
- overpolished
- overly symmetrical
- too many abstractions in a row
- too much obvious persuasion energy
- sounding committee-written

### Example
Less aligned:
> We help local businesses elevate their digital presence and convert more visitors into loyal clients.

More aligned:
> A few focused improvements could likely make the site stronger, clearer, and easier for people to act on.

---

## 6. Outreach-specific guidance

### First touch should usually contain
1. one reason for writing
2. one specific observation
3. one opportunity statement
4. one low-pressure next step

### First touch should usually avoid
- detailed teardown energy
- too many findings
- strong diagnosis language
- direct meeting ask
- fake warmth

### Preferred closes
- “If useful, I’d be happy to send a few concise ideas.”
- “If it’d be helpful, I’m happy to send a few specific notes.”
- “If useful, I can send over a short version of what stood out.”

---

## 7. Deck / positioning guidance

When writing decks or marketing materials, Daymian/Kestrel should sound:
- commercially literate
- visually literate
- calm
- non-hypey
- lightly opinionated

Decks should feel like:
- observations from someone with standards
- not positioning exercises built by a consultancy

Preferred themes:
- trust
- clarity
- first impression
- next-step ease
- better representation of the business
- getting more out of what’s already there

---

## 8. Blind spot guidance

Daymian’s likely blind spots should influence how future writing is reviewed.

### Blind spot 1: rejecting anything that feels even slightly artificial
Countermeasure:
- ask not only “is this cringe?” but also “is this still clear and effective?”

### Blind spot 2: overcorrecting toward understatement
Countermeasure:
- make sure the writing still carries a business case and does not become too quiet to matter

### Blind spot 3: refining too long before shipping
Countermeasure:
- favor versions that feel true enough and useful enough, even if not perfectly polished

### Blind spot 4: preferring elegance over straightforward persuasion
Countermeasure:
- keep the upside legible
- make sure the reader still understands why this matters commercially

---

## 9. Best final test

Before sending a draft, ask:

- Does this sound like a real person?
- Does it sound like I actually looked?
- Does it preserve the recipient’s dignity?
- Does it make the upside clear without sounding pushy?
- Does it feel like me?
- Would I cringe hearing this read out loud?

If the answer is yes across those, it is probably close.

---

## 10. One-line guide

Daymian should sound like a thoughtful, high-standard, low-bullshit business owner who can see where a good business could be represented more clearly — and who talks about that opportunity with calm confidence rather than sales pressure.
