# Daymian / Kestrel — Non-Negotiables Before Sending

Use this as a final quick-check before sending important outreach, brand copy, or client-facing writing.

---

## Non-negotiables

### 1. It has to sound like a real person
If it sounds automated, committee-written, or over-optimized, stop.

### 2. It cannot feel like a solicitor script
No classic cold-outreach tone.
No “I help businesses grow” sludge.
No pitch cadence.

### 3. It should preserve the recipient’s dignity
Do not make the reader feel judged, embarrassed, or corrected in the first touch.

### 4. Lead with opportunity, not criticism
Frame the work as upside:
- stronger
- clearer
- more effective
- more trust-building
- easier to act on

Not as:
- old
- weak
- broken
- outdated

### 5. Specificity matters
It should sound like I actually looked.
One real observation beats five generic ones.

### 6. Keep the business case visible
Even restrained writing should still answer:
- why this matters
- why it could be worth spending money on
- how it could help the business do more

### 7. No agency boilerplate
Avoid:
- online presence
- optimize conversions
- unlock growth
- elevate your brand
- scale your business
- digital ecosystem

### 8. No fake warmth
Avoid performative friendliness or generic flattery.
If it sounds like a mass email, cut it.

### 9. Don’t overexplain Kestrel
The brand should feel legible through tone.
It does not need a paragraph of positioning in the first message.

### 10. Keep local groundedness subtle
Usually better in the signature than in the body.

Preferred signature:

<name>  
Kestrel Labs  
Atlanta, Georgia

### 11. No meeting ask too early
First touch should usually end with a low-pressure offer:
- “If useful, I’d be happy to send a few concise ideas.”

### 12. Understated is good — but not so understated it loses the point
Make sure the upside is still clear.
Quiet is good.
Too quiet is not.

### 13. Keep the plainspoken/direct quality
Do not polish it so much that it stops sounding like a real operator.
Useful beats elegant.

### 14. Do not send anything I would cringe hearing out loud
If it sounds false when spoken, it is probably wrong.


---

## Final send test

Before sending, ask:

- Is this true?
- Is this specific?
- Is this respectful?
- Is this commercially useful?
- Does it sound like me?
- Would I be comfortable receiving this?
- Does it avoid sounding like everyone else?

If not, revise.

---

## One-line reminder

Be sharp, real, respectful, and useful — never mass-produced, never pushy, never fake.
